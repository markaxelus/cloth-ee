# Cloth-ee pitch and live demo

## The story

Fashion sustainability usually starts too late. We calculate impact after a garment has been made, bought, and discarded. But waste often begins earlier—with a post, a trend, a low price, or information that was never shown.

Cloth-ee makes that invisible chain visible.

It is a synthetic town of 40 residents with different budgets, tastes, jobs, social connections, and shopping habits. Every purchase follows transparent rules. Residents wear garments, influence one another, chase trends, buy copies, resell, donate, or throw clothing away. We can trace one post all the way to the garments it displaced and the usable wears that were lost.

Then we change one signal and rerun the exact same town. What happens if a brand publishes a product passport? What if clothing lasts longer? What if resale is easier? Cloth-ee does not claim to predict a real person. It is a scenario lab for asking which interventions are worth testing in the real world.

## 90-second demo

**0–15 seconds — the problem**

“A garment does not become waste at the bin. Sometimes it becomes waste the moment a trend convinces someone to replace clothing that still has life left.”

Point to the live town and the Cost of influence counters.

**15–35 seconds — one causal story**

Select a resident with a highlighted trend chain.

“Every resident is synthetic, but every decision is traceable. This purchase came from a social post. It pushed out another garment with usable wears remaining. We show the signal, the decision, and the consequence—not just a carbon score.”

**35–60 seconds — the experiment**

Run **Compare 14 simulated days**.

“Now we duplicate the town: same residents, same random seed, same 14 days. In the second town, one brand publishes its clothing passport. That is the only changed signal.”

Read the result exactly as shown, even if the effect is zero or unexpected.

**60–78 seconds — grounded intelligence**

“The simulation remains deterministic and testable. OMNI is used on demand to inspect what is visibly worn and explain it; it does not secretly decide how residents behave. Missing lifecycle evidence stays unknown, and sourced estimates retain their system boundary.”

Open a resident’s model capture or use Scan with OMNI only if the live endpoint has already passed its smoke test.

**78–90 seconds — close**

“Cloth-ee turns sustainability from a static label into a question we can test: which signals change behavior, for whom, and at what cost? Before spending years on an intervention, we can see the mechanism, challenge the assumptions, and decide what deserves a real-world trial.”

## One-line pitch

**Cloth-ee is a transparent simulation lab that traces how fashion influence becomes clothing waste—and tests which interventions could change the outcome.**

## What makes it credible

- The same seed and settings replay deterministically.
- Decisions are rule-based and inspectable, not hidden inside an LLM.
- The demo distinguishes simulated events, calculated estimates, and unknown data.
- Trend cascades connect posts to purchases, premature replacement, and disposal.
- OMNI sees rendered pixels rather than receiving hidden outfit ground truth.
- Product passports affect only residents whose modeled preferences value evidence.

## If judges challenge the premise

**“Is this predicting real people?”**  
No. It is a synthetic scenario model. Its purpose is to expose mechanisms and compare interventions before real-world validation.

**“Are these environmental numbers real?”**  
Only a number backed by a sourced factor is displayed. Missing factors stay unknown. Every estimate states its boundary.

**“Why use AI?”**  
OMNI lets a user inspect a resident from visible pixels and ask natural-language questions. The simulation itself stays deterministic, cheap, and testable.

**“What would happen next?”**  
Calibrate persona weights and transition rates with merchant, survey, and returns data; run controlled real-world trials; then compare observed behavior with the simulated hypothesis.

## Demo safety

- Start on `/town.html`, not the fitting-room page.
- Use the local simulation as the guaranteed path.
- Run the comparison once before presenting and know the exact result.
- Do not depend on live OMNI unless the endpoint is already verified.
- Never call synthetic residents “customers” or scenario deltas “predictions.”
